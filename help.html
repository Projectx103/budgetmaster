Help & FAQ

How can we help you?

Quick answers to common questions about envelope budgeting, accounts, transactions, and how every number is calculated.

Dashboard

TBB is money you've received but haven't assigned to any category yet. Your goal is to bring this to ₱0 every month by giving every peso a job — that's the heart of envelope budgeting.

Example You earn ₱20,000. Before you assign anything, TBB = ₱20,000. You assign ₱8,000 to Rent, ₱5,000 to Groceries, ₱2,000 to Transport. TBB is now ₱5,000 — that's still unassigned money you can use anywhere.

They sound similar but mean very different things:

  • TBB = money not yet assigned to any category. Should be ₱0 when you're done budgeting.
  • Available Balance = total income minus total spending. It's your real spendable money this month.
Example Income: ₱20,000 · Assigned to categories: ₱20,000 · Spent so far: ₱5,000. TBB = ₱0 (you assigned everything) · Available Balance = ₱15,000 (still in your accounts).

A category goes red when your spent amount exceeds your assigned amount. It's not an error — it's a normal part of budgeting that happens to everyone.

At month-end rollover, you'll be asked how to handle each overspent category:

  • Cover next month — the overspent amount carries forward as a negative balance. You'll need to assign new money next month to clear it.
  • Absorb into pool — the deficit is taken out of your total TBB pool, and the category resets to ₱0.

Accounts

Each account transaction type affects different totals. Here's a clear breakdown:

  • Deposit Account balance ↑ · Available Balance ↑ · TBB ↑ · Doesn't affect any category
  • Withdraw Account balance ↓ · Available Balance ↓ · TBB ↓ · Doesn't affect any category
  • Pay (credit card) Asset account ↓ · Credit card balance owed ↓ · Available Balance ↓ · TBB unchanged
  • Transfer One account ↓ · Another account ↑ · Available Balance unchanged · TBB unchanged
  • Expense Account balance ↓ · Category spent ↑ · Available Balance ↓ · TBB unchanged

Credit cards work in two steps:

Step 1 — You charge something to the card. Use the Expense button inside that credit card account. This:

  • Increases your credit card balance (you now owe more)
  • Increases the spent amount in the linked budget category (e.g. Groceries)
  • Does NOT decrease any cash account — you haven't paid yet

Step 2 — You pay the credit card bill. Use the Pay button. This:

  • Decreases the asset account you paid from (e.g. Checking ↓)
  • Decreases the credit card balance owed (you now owe less)
  • Does NOT affect any budget category — that was already counted in Step 1

Transfer = moving money between your own accounts. It doesn't change your total wealth, so Available Balance and TBB stay the same.

Deposit = new money entering your accounts from outside (salary, gift, allowance). It increases your total wealth, so TBB goes up.

Right way to use each • Moving ₱5,000 from BDO Savings to GCash → Transfer • Your boss paid your salary into BDO Savings → Deposit • Mom sent you ₱2,000 via GCash → Deposit

A Withdrawal means money is leaving your tracked accounts without being attached to a budget category — like converting savings to physical cash you'll spend untracked, or sending money to someone outside your budget system.

Since the money is gone from your wealth and not tied to any category's spent amount, it must come out of TBB to keep the budget math correct.

Tip If you're paying for something specific, use Expense instead — it lets you categorise the spending properly.

Budget

Open the Budget section, find your category, and tap the assigned amount to edit it. The number you enter is moved from TBB into that category.

You can only assign money you have. If you try to assign more than your current TBB, the app will block it and show an error — you need more income first.

Envelope budgeting only works if you're assigning real money you actually have. Allowing you to assign more than your income would let you plan to spend money that doesn't exist, which defeats the whole point.

If you want to assign more to a category, you need to either:

  • Add more income to this month, or
  • Reduce the amount assigned to another category to free up TBB

Yes. Real life doesn't fit a perfect budget — you might spend more on groceries than you assigned. BudgetMaster will allow it as long as your total spending doesn't exceed your total income.

The overspent category will show in red, and at rollover time you'll decide how to handle the deficit — Cover next month or Absorb from TBB.

Example Groceries assigned: ₱5,000 · Groceries spent: ₱5,800 → overspent by ₱800. That's fine if you have ₱800+ unassigned in TBB or another category — but you'll need to clear that deficit eventually.

Rollover

Rollover is the process of closing the current month and opening the next one. It carries forward leftover money in each category so unspent savings aren't lost.

You can trigger it manually from the Rollover button, or set it to run automatically on a chosen day of the month in Settings.

It carries forward automatically. Each category's new starting amount = its previous assigned - spent value.

Example Groceries: assigned ₱5,000, spent ₱4,200 → carries forward ₱800 to next month. New month starts with Groceries assigned = ₱800, spent = ₱0.

When a category is overspent at rollover, you get two choices for how to handle it:

  • Cover next month — the category starts with a negative balance equal to the overspent amount. You must assign new money next month to bring it back to ₱0+.
  • Absorb into pool — the category resets to ₱0, and the deficit is taken out of next month's TBB pool. This is like saying "the rest of my budget covers this overspend."
Example Groceries overspent by ₱500.
• Cover → next month, Groceries starts at -₱500 (you owe it back).
• Absorb → next month, Groceries starts at ₱0 and TBB is reduced by ₱500.

Not directly — rollovers create permanent month boundaries. But all your data is preserved in the Reports section, and the previous month's transactions, categories, and balances are still viewable via the month selector.

If a rollover was triggered by mistake, you can:

  • Navigate back to the previous month using the month picker arrows
  • Check the rolloverHistory log in your data for the exact carry-forward amounts
  • Manually adjust assignments in the new month to undo any unwanted changes
